# Lore and Identity

Narrative supports gameplay; it does not define mechanics independently of gameplay needs.

## Player

The player is an arcane researcher exploring and extending a system of magic.

Core identity:

* experimenter
* observer
* creator
* knowledge seeker

The fantasy is discovery through understanding, not prophecy, equipment collection or conventional heroic power progression.

## Tower

The Tower is a magical research archive, laboratory and containment structure shaped by past experimentation.

It provides a fiction for:

* changing environments
* dangerous experiments
* magical discoveries
* enemies/anomalies
* research progression

The Tower supports the spell-creation fantasy; it is not more important than that fantasy.

## Magic

Magic is treated as a system that can be studied and recombined.

The player discovers principles and interactions rather than predefined complete spells.

Past researchers left partial knowledge, experiments and consequences rather than a catalog of finished abilities.

## Research Domains

Possible thematic domains include:

* fragmentation
* thermal conversion
* spatial manipulation
* temporal distortion

Domains provide thematic/content direction, not mandatory mechanical categories.

## Instability

Instability represents approaching the limits of magical control.

Themes:

* possibility
* complexity
* risk
* incomplete understanding

It should feel like pushing an experiment too far, not arbitrary punishment.

## Theme

Central question:

> How far can magic be pushed before understanding is lost?

Narrative content should reinforce experimentation, discovery and ownership of created magic.
