# Unbound Arcana - Core Gameplay Loop

## Game Identity

Unbound Arcana is a 2D top-down pixel art roguelike focused on player-created magic through a composition system.

The core fantasy is:

> Create a spell nobody else could have imagined.

The player does not collect predefined spells.

The player creates magical systems by combining:

* Behaviors
* Principles
* Runtime interactions
* Events
* Environmental effects

The game is about experimentation and discovery.

The player is not searching for the strongest weapon.

The player is researching the limits of magic.

---

# Player Identity

The player is an arcane researcher.

They are not:

* A chosen hero
* A traditional wizard
* A character collecting spells
* An equipment optimizer

They are someone experimenting with a magical system beyond its original limitations.

The player creates theories, tests them, observes results, and adapts.

---

# Core Gameplay Loop

The primary gameplay cycle:

```
Create Experiment
        ↓
Enter Tower
        ↓
Test Spell Against Reality
        ↓
Discover New Magical Principles
        ↓
Modify Experiment
        ↓
Face More Complex Challenges
        ↓
Extract Knowledge
        ↓
Create New Experiments
```

The run is a research expedition.

---

# Spell Creation Philosophy

The player does not find:

"Fireball"

The player creates:

"Projectile + Thermal Principle + Expansion + Fragmentation"

The spell is the result of experimentation.

---

# Spell Structure

A spell is composed of several layers.

## Behavior

The fundamental identity of the spell.

Examples:

* Projectile
* Beam
* Aura
* Minion
* Sentinel
* Zone

A behavior defines what kind of magical object exists.

---

## Principles

Principles modify behaviors.

Examples:

* Fire
* Explosion
* Split
* Homing
* Chain
* Movement alterations
* Size alterations

Principles should create emergent combinations rather than predefined recipes.

---

## Spell Evolution

Spells are expected to evolve during a run.

A player should feel:

"I created this."

Not:

"I replaced my old weapon with a stronger one."

Example:

Beginning:

Projectile

↓

Projectile + Fire

↓

Projectile + Fire + Split

↓

Projectile + Fire + Split + Explosion

The experiment becomes increasingly complex.

---

# Spell Slots

Current design hypothesis:

The player maintains a small number of meaningful experiments.

Baseline:

## Active Spells

2 slots.

Examples:

* Projectile
* Beam
* Aura
* Minion
* Sentinel

These are deliberate magical constructs used by the player.

---

## Passive Spell

1 slot.

Passive spells are not simple buffs.

They are persistent magical systems.

Examples:

* Defensive fields
* Reality distortions
* Persistent magical effects

---

Additional slots may exist through special progression, but should remain limited.

The goal is not to create an inventory system.

The goal is to maintain a few complex experiments.

---

# Spell Modification

Spell modification should happen through meaningful research moments.

Preferred loop:

```
Initial Configuration
        ↓
Combat Trial
        ↓
Discovery
        ↓
Research / Experimentation
        ↓
Harder Combat Trial
```

Discoveries can be collected during exploration, but major changes happen at dedicated experimentation points.

This reinforces the researcher fantasy.

---

# Deterministic Magic

Spell combinations should have predictable results.

The player should be rewarded for understanding the magical system.

Examples:

Projectile + Explosion always creates an explosive projectile.

Projectile + Fire + Split always creates a fire splitting projectile.

The complexity should come from combinations and interactions, not from the spell randomly changing identity.

---

# Instability

Instability represents the limits of magical control.

It is not simply a punishment.

The strongest experiments should often require accepting instability.

A powerful spell may be:

* More complex
* More dangerous
* More difficult to control

Instability should create meaningful decisions:

"Can I push this experiment further?"

Possible future direction:

* Weighted unstable events
* Positive and negative outcomes
* Category-based effects
* Player awareness of risk

Instability should create stories, not frustration.

---

# Progression Philosophy

Permanent progression is knowledge.

The player unlocks:

* New behaviors
* New principles
* New interactions
* New magical possibilities

The player should not primarily gain:

* Permanent damage increases
* Traditional equipment upgrades
* Endless numerical scaling

Experienced players are stronger because they understand more of the magical language.

---

# Run Philosophy

A run represents a research expedition.

Target duration:

Failed run:
15-25 minutes

Successful run:
30-40 minutes

The run should provide enough time for experiments to evolve while still allowing meaningful resets.

---

# Power Philosophy

Spell strength is not only damage.

A spell can be powerful through:

## Output

Damage and direct impact.

## Control

Manipulating enemies and space.

## Adaptability

Handling different situations.

## Interaction Potential

Creating complex magical systems.

## Risk Capacity

Accepting instability to achieve greater effects.

The most memorable spells are not necessarily the strongest.

They are the ones that create unexpected solutions.
